# How to do overlapping

<p class="lead">Overlapping means reading the text (the script) aloud while matching the audio exactly. It trains you to imitate how sounds link together and the rhythm of speech, and it is often used as a step before shadowing.</p>

## The steps of overlapping

1. Make sure you understand the text. Look up unfamiliar words and structures first
2. Read at the same time as the audio. Stay with the audio, neither falling behind nor getting ahead
3. Repeat the sentences where you got out of sync. Go back to the sentence where you couldn't keep up, and practice just that part
4. Once you can match it easily, raise the speed: try the original speed, then a little faster

## What you need

Overlapping needs audio and its text. Audio that comes without a text can't be used for it as is.

## Overlapping with Reprise

Reprise transcribes the audio you import on your iPhone, and you can use that transcript as your script.

1. Import audio. It is split into sentences, with text for each
2. Set the playback style to “Play Through.” The sentence currently playing is highlighted, so follow it with your eyes as you speak along
3. Tap a sentence where you got out of sync to hear it again from the start, or repeat it with “Loop 1 Sentence”
4. You can adjust the speed between 0.5× and 2.0× without changing the pitch

The transcript can contain mistakes. When you notice one, touch and hold the sentence and fix the text in “Adjust This Sentence.”

If you only have a text and no audio, paste the text into “Create from Text” and an on-device voice reads it aloud to make the audio. In this case the text and the audio match exactly.

## The next step

Once you can match the audio, hide the text and move on to [shadowing](/en/guides/shadowing).
