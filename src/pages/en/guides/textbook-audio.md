# How to practice with textbook audio, one sentence at a time

<p class="lead">The audio that comes with textbooks and workbooks is the easiest material to practice with: the text and translations are all there, and you chose the level to suit you. But in an ordinary music player, going back to a sentence in the middle of a track is a hassle, and you often end up just listening.</p>

## What gets in the way when you practice with textbook audio

- A single track holds dozens of sentences, and it's hard to get back to the start of the one you want to hear again
- There is no pause for speaking, so you end up pressing play and pause over and over
- Nothing keeps track of which tracks you practiced and how far you got

## Importing downloaded audio

This is for books whose audio you can download as mp3 or other files from the publisher's website.

1. Save the audio to the Files app on your iPhone. If it comes as a zip file, tap it in the Files app to unzip it
2. In Reprise, on the Materials screen, tap “+” and choose “From Files (Audio or Video)”
3. Select all the tracks of the book together. Files you select at once become one material, sorted by file name
4. Open a track and it is transcribed on your iPhone and split into sentences

If you downloaded the audio on a computer, put it in iCloud Drive or similar, and you can select it from the Files app on your iPhone.

## Importing CD audio

For books that come with a CD, you'll need a computer.

1. Import the CD with the Music app on your computer (iTunes or similar on Windows) and sync it to your iPhone
2. In Reprise, tap “+”, choose “From Music Library” and select the album
3. Each album becomes one material, in the CD's track order

You can import songs that are downloaded to your iPhone and have no DRM (copy protection). Audio imported from a CD qualifies.

## How to practice

- First, listen with the text hidden. For sentences you couldn't catch, touch and hold the eye button to check the text
- Speak out loud. With “Pause After Each Sentence,” each sentence is followed by a pause matched to its length. Repeat until you can say it without looking at the book ([repeating](/en/guides/repeating))
- Once you're used to it, follow the audio without pauses ([shadowing](/en/guides/shadowing))
- Bookmark the sentences that are hard to say. Swipe a sentence to the right to mark it, and you can review them together later
- For fast audio, start around 0.8×. The pitch doesn't change

If you keep one book as one material, your progress and study time for each track are recorded, so you can see how far you've come.

## Good to know

- The text is transcribed by speech recognition and may differ from the text in the book. Touch and hold a sentence and fix it with “Adjust This Sentence”
- Speech recognition works in one language at a time. If the audio includes explanations or translations read in your own language, those parts won't be transcribed correctly. Touch and hold a sentence and choose “Recognize Again” to recognize just that sentence in a different language
- Parts with read-out question numbers or sound effects may not be split well. Touch and hold a sentence to merge it, split it again or adjust its boundaries
- Audio that can only be played inside a publisher's own app can't be imported. Audio you can download as files, or audio from a CD, is what you can use
- Use the bundled audio for your own study. If the publisher sets terms of use, follow them
- You can keep up to 5 materials for free. The audio you import never leaves your device
